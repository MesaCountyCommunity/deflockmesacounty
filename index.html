// What A Surveillance Ordinance Does

Make it permanent. Three things.

Ending a contract removes one vendor. It does not change the process that let the contract get signed without a public hearing, and it does not stop the next company from selling the county something worse. The rule has to be written down.

  1. Pass a surveillance oversight ordinance. Community Control Over Police Surveillance (CCOPS) means no surveillance technology is acquired or deployed until the elected body votes on it in a public hearing; every system has a written use policy covering who may access the data, for what purposes, and how long it is kept; and the department publishes an annual report on how each technology was actually used and every outside agency the data went to.
  2. Ban facial recognition outright. Not a use policy — a prohibition. Face surveillance identifies people by their bodies rather than their plates, and it misidentifies women and people of color at measurably higher rates. TODO: check what Colorado already restricts here (the state has acted on facial recognition by government agencies) and cite it rather than borrowing another state's examples.
  3. No vendor-owned surveillance. If a local agency ever deploys surveillance equipment, the agency owns the hardware and the data, the data lives on government systems, and no private company decides who else gets to search it.

// Can't Make it to a Meeting?

Send An Email.

Showing up to a meeting is still the most effective thing you can do. But a clear, specific email from a constituent is a real action too, and it takes about two minutes.

Pick your jurisdiction, choose who should hear from you, and this builds the message. Nothing is sent from this site and no address is collected — the draft opens in your own email app so it arrives from you.

01 / PICK
Choose your city or the county, then tap the officials who should hear from you.
02 / WRITE
Start from a campaign template and edit it. A message in your own words counts for far more than a form letter.
03 / SEND
Open it in your email app and send. Or copy the recipients, subject, and body by hand.
