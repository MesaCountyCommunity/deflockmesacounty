// What We Are Working Toward

DeFlock Mesa County's Goals

1
End the ALPR Contracts

TODO: name each Mesa County agency under contract and when that contract renews — the renewal date is the deadline that makes this section urgent. Then cite the communities that have already walked away.
2
Pass a CCOPS Ordinance

Ending a contract with Flock removes one vendor- it does not change the process that let the contract be signed without a public hearing, and it does not stop the next company from selling the county something worse. The rule has to be written down.

Community Control Over Police Surveillance (CCOPS) means no surveillance technology is acquired or deployed until the elected body votes on it in a public hearing; every system has a written use policy covering who may access the data, for what purposes, and how long it is kept; and the department publishes an annual report on how each technology was actually used and every outside agency the data went to.

3
Ban Privately-controlled Surveillance

No privately-owned surveillance. If a local agency ever deploys surveillance equipment, the agency owns the hardware and the data, the data lives on government systems, and no private company decides who else gets to search it.
